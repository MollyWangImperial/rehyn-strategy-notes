# Yield curve steepener trade

`c2` · Macro and carry · Government bonds and rates

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Positioning for Curve Steepening

The investment case rests on the tendency of short-term Treasury yields to fall faster than long-term yields when the Federal Reserve starts cutting rates, because short-term yields follow the policy rate while long-term yields reflect growth, inflation and the supply of debt. The pattern persists because central banks cut when growth weakens, while large government deficits and heavy Treasury issuance keep investors demanding extra yield for holding long-dated bonds, and trade policy and geopolitical risks can raise that demand further.

The main risk is that the curve flattens, which happens when the Federal Reserve raises rates faster than expected or when long-term bonds rally on safe haven buying. In 2022, short-term yields rose above long-term yields as inflation forced rapid rate rises, and the position lost for months. A strategy that is correct on the direction of rates can therefore still lose, because its return depends on the gap between maturities rather than on the level of yields.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the Federal Reserve's target policy rate during the episode, in basis points, where 100 basis points equal one percentage point. The strategy tends to gain when expected cuts pull short-term yields down faster than long-term yields, and to lose when rate rises lift short-term yields above them.

**January 2008 · Recession fears · −125bp · strategy gained**  
Emergency cuts steepen the Treasury curve  
The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 125 basis points in January 2008, including an emergency cut, as recession risks grew. The strategy gained because the 2-year yield fell about 1 percentage point, more than twice the fall in the 10-year yield.

**December 2008 · Quantitative easing · −75bp · strategy lost**  
Bond purchases pull long-term yields lower  
The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate to a range of zero to 0.25% in December 2008 after announcing mortgage bond purchases, which pulled the 10-year yield down to 2.2%. The strategy lost because the 10-year yield fell by more than the 2-year yield.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −150bp · strategy mixed**  
Covid emergency cuts meet a Treasury sell-off  
The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 150 basis points in March 2020, while the 10-year yield rose from 0.5% to 1.3% in under two weeks. Results were mixed because the curve steepened but wider bid-ask spreads reduced the gain.

**June 2022 · Inflation · +75bp · strategy lost**  
Fed rate rise flattens the Treasury curve  
The Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by 75 basis points in June 2022, the largest rise since 1994, as the war in Ukraine kept energy prices high. The strategy lost because the 2-year yield rose above the 10-year yield.

**March 2023 · Banking stress · +25bp · strategy gained**  
Bank failures drag short-term yields lower  
The failure of Silicon Valley Bank in March 2023 pulled the 2-year yield down by about 1 percentage point in three days, while the Fed still raised rates. The strategy gained because short-term yields fell faster than long-term yields.

**September 2024 · Rate cuts · −50bp · strategy gained**  
First Fed cut since 2020 steepens the curve  
The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 50 basis points in September 2024, its first cut since 2020, as the 2-year yield fell below the 10-year yield. The strategy gained as the curve moved from inversion to a positive slope.

### Today’s market watch

The gap between 2-year and 10-year Treasury yields, the policy rate path priced by the market and the level of long-term yields relative to inflation expectations are the main indicators for this strategy. Rehyn watches labour market data and Federal Reserve meetings because they change the expected pace of rate cuts. Large Treasury auctions and fiscal policy announcements also matter because higher long-term yields can steepen the curve through the 10-year leg.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Expected central bank rate cuts**: Expected rate cuts favour the strategy because they lower short-term yields, which follow the policy rate, by more than long-term yields, which depend on growth and inflation over a decade. In January 2008 the Fed cut by 125 basis points, and the 2-year yield fell more than twice as much as the 10-year yield.
- **Rising term premium on long bonds**: A higher term premium favours the strategy because investors demand more compensation for holding long-dated bonds, which lifts the 10-year yield relative to the 2-year yield. In April 2025, concern about US trade policy and the budget deficit pushed the 10-year yield up by about 50 basis points in a week, while the 2-year yield rose by less.
- **Banking or growth scares**: A banking or growth scare favours the strategy because investors expect the Fed to cut quickly, which lowers 2-year yields faster than 10-year yields. In March 2023 the 2-year yield fell about 1 percentage point in three days, while the 10-year yield fell by about 0.4.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Rapid rate rises by the Fed**: Rapid policy tightening hurts the strategy because short-term yields rise above long-term yields, which inverts the curve. In 2022 the Fed raised its policy rate by 425 basis points, so the 2s10s spread fell from about 80 basis points to about minus 50.
- **Rallies in long-dated bonds**: A rally in long-dated bonds hurts the strategy because the 10-year yield falls by more than the 2-year yield, which flattens the curve. In December 2008, bond purchases by the Fed pulled the 10-year yield down by about 70 basis points in a month, while the 2-year yield fell by about 20.
- **Oil price shocks lifting inflation**: A supply shock that lifts oil prices hurts the strategy because higher inflation delays rate cuts and raises short-term yields. In March 2022, Brent crude rose above 120 dollars after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, the 2-year yield rose about 80 basis points in the month and the curve flattened.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Interest Rate Returns?

- **Yield curve butterfly trading** (`r5`): Trades kinks along the curve while staying neutral to its level and slope. Bears model risk, losing when kinks widen further. *Earns from curve shape, not slope*
- **Fixed income trend following** (`m7`): Follows rate trends in either direction using bond futures. Bears reversals, losing when trends break down. *Profits when rate cycles run further*
- **Long-duration Treasury hedge** (`d6`): Holds long Treasuries outright. Bears inflation and rising yields, losing when stocks and bonds fall together. *Holds duration, not curve slope*
