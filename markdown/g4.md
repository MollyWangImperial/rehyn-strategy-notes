# Core digital asset allocation

`g4` · Growth · Crypto

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Long-Term Digital Asset Adoption

The investment case rests on the continued adoption of scarce digital assets as stores of value, settlement networks and collateral, allowing a diversified core allocation to participate in growth across the crypto market. The opportunity persists because network adoption remains early, institutional access is expanding, and governments are developing different rules for ownership, taxation and payments, creating recurring policy-driven repricing.

The main risk is extreme price volatility, with annualised volatility around 48% and past cycle drawdowns exceeding 70% as leverage, liquidity and investor confidence reversed together. Losses can deepen when exchange failures or tighter regulation force investors to reduce exposure across otherwise distinct assets. Positive return skew does not make the allocation defensive because Bitcoin and Ether remain closely correlated during broad crypto sell-offs.

**March 2013 · Capital controls · +172% · strategy gained**  
Cyprus Controls Accelerate Bitcoin Demand  
Cyprus imposed bank controls during its financial rescue, increasing interest in assets held outside the banking system as Bitcoin rose 172%. The strategy gained because demand for Bitcoin broadened while the crypto market remained small and supply was limited.

**December 2017 · Retail demand · +39% · strategy gained**  
Retail Demand Drives Bitcoin Higher  
Retail participation and new futures contracts pushed Bitcoin up 39% during the final stage of its 2017 rally. The strategy gained because demand expanded faster than market liquidity, while Ether also benefited from initial coin offering activity.

**March 2020 · Funding stress · −25% · strategy lost**  
Global Cash Rush Hits Digital Assets  
The global rush for cash pushed Bitcoin down 25% as leveraged positions were liquidated across crypto exchanges. The strategy lost because digital assets traded as high-risk assets rather than safe havens, while Ether fell more sharply than Bitcoin.

**May 2021 · China policy · −35% · strategy lost**  
China Crackdown Reverses Crypto Rally  
China intensified restrictions on crypto trading and mining as Bitcoin fell 35% from an already extended valuation. The strategy lost because regulatory pressure prompted forced selling, reduced mining activity and weakened confidence across the digital asset market.

**November 2022 · Exchange failure · −16% · strategy lost**  
FTX Failure Deepens Crypto Losses  
The failure of FTX pushed Bitcoin down 16% as customers withdrew assets and lenders reduced exposure to the sector. The strategy lost because counterparty concerns spread across exchanges, while forced liquidations weighed on Bitcoin and Ether together.

**November 2024 · US policy · +37% · strategy gained**  
US Election Lifts Policy Expectations  
The US election raised expectations for clearer digital asset rules and pushed Bitcoin up 37%. The strategy gained because investors anticipated broader institutional access, while strong exchange-traded fund flows supported demand for the largest assets.

Institutional fund flows, stablecoin supply, exchange leverage and Bitcoin’s share of the crypto market indicate whether demand is broadening or becoming dependent on speculative positioning. ## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Institutional access expands**: Broader institutional access favours the strategy because regulated custody and exchange-traded products reduce operational barriers and bring new capital into a market with limited liquid supply. US spot Bitcoin fund approvals in January 2024 preceded strong inflows and a renewed rise in major digital assets.
- **Monetary conditions ease**: Easier monetary conditions favour the strategy because lower real yields support long-duration assets and make speculative capital more available. Bitcoin and Ether rose strongly after the policy response to the 2020 recession increased global liquidity and reduced interest rates.
- **Network use broadens**: Rising network use favours the strategy because higher settlement activity, stablecoin adoption and developer participation strengthen demand for block space and collateral. Ether benefited during 2020 and 2021 as decentralised finance and stablecoin activity expanded on its network.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Liquidity tightens rapidly**: Tighter liquidity hurts the strategy because higher real yields reduce demand for assets that produce no contractual cash flow and increase the cost of leveraged positions. Aggressive Federal Reserve tightening in 2022 coincided with a broad decline in Bitcoin, Ether and crypto credit.
- **Regulation restricts market access**: Restrictive regulation hurts the strategy because limits on trading, banking or custody reduce demand and can force miners and intermediaries to relocate. China’s 2021 restrictions on mining and trading contributed to a 35% monthly fall in Bitcoin.
- **Counterparty failures force selling**: Counterparty failures hurt the strategy because frozen withdrawals and impaired collateral spread losses across exchanges, lenders and investment funds. The collapse of FTX in November 2022 drove simultaneous declines in Bitcoin and Ether despite their separate networks.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Digital Asset Returns?

- **Crypto trend following** (`m6`): Follows crypto price trends and bears reversal risk rather than full-cycle market exposure. *Adapts exposure as trends change*
- **Crypto basis trading** (`r2`): Earns futures basis and bears exchange, funding and convergence risk instead of directional market risk. *Uses pricing gaps within crypto*
- **Crypto volatility arbitrage** (`s7`): Trades differences in crypto volatility and bears model, liquidity and tail risk. *Targets volatility rather than adoption*
