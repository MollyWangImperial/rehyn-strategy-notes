# Long gamma scalping

`s4` · Short term volatility · Options

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Profiting from Realised Volatility

The investment case rests on the tendency of large market moves to cluster, so a holder of options who hedges frequently can earn more from realised moves than the options cost. The opportunity persists because insurers, structured product issuers and yield-seeking funds supply options at prices based on average conditions, while geopolitical and policy events produce bursts of movement that those averages understate.

The main risk is a steady loss of option premium through time decay when markets move less than the options imply. Each hedge also costs a small amount in spreads and slippage, so a long period of calm can produce losses over many months. Rehyn estimates that a prolonged low-volatility period, such as 2017, is the most damaging condition because gains from hedging arrive only when moves are large.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the highest closing level of the VIX during the episode. The VIX measures the volatility that S&P 500 options price in for the next 30 days: it has averaged about 19 over the long run, while readings above 30 signal market stress.

**August 2011 · Policy · 48 · strategy gained**  
US credit downgrade drives large daily moves  
Standard and Poor's downgraded the US credit rating on 5 August 2011, and the VIX closed at 48 on 8 August. The strategy gained because the S&P 500 fell about 6.7% that day and further large daily moves followed.

**June 2016 · Referendum · 26 · strategy mixed**  
UK vote to leave the European Union  
The UK vote of 23 June 2016 to leave the European Union lifted the VIX to a 26 close as the S&P 500 fell about 3.6%. Results were mixed because the market recovered within days, so time decay offset part of the hedging gain.

**August 2017 · Geopolitics · 16 · strategy lost**  
North Korea tensions fail to sustain volatility  
Threats between the US and North Korea lifted the VIX to a 16 close on 10 August 2017, when the S&P 500 fell about 1.5%. The strategy lost because the market recovered within days and realised volatility stayed below the level priced into options.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · 83 · strategy gained**  
Covid turns into a global funding crisis  
The March 2020 sell-off turned a health emergency into a global rush for cash, lifting the VIX to an 83 close on 16 March. The strategy gained because the S&P 500 fell about 12% that day and moves above 5% recurred through the month.

**August 2024 · Carry trade · 39 · strategy mixed**  
Bank of Japan rate rise unwinds yen trades  
A Bank of Japan rate rise and weak US jobs data forced yen-funded trades to close, and the VIX closed at 39 on 5 August. Results were mixed because the S&P 500 recovered within two weeks, so time decay offset part of the gain.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · 52 · strategy gained**  
US tariffs drive the largest spike since 2020  
US tariff announcements lifted the VIX to a 52 close on 8 April 2025, after the S&P 500 fell about 6% on 4 April and rose about 9.5% on 9 April. The strategy gained because the options paid for large moves in both directions.

## Quick backtest across 16 years of market shocks

Return 9.4% · volatility 7.6% · Sharpe 0.78 · worst fall −9% (November 2017)

- Period: January 2010 to December 2025; 192 monthly observations
- Instrument: S&P 500 index options and E-mini S&P 500 futures; about 30 days to expiry; hedged intraday and rolled monthly
- Rules: At-the-money straddles bought when implied volatility is below forecast realised volatility; delta hedged with futures at each 0.25% move in the index; options closed before expiry
- Costs: Estimated option spreads, futures commissions and slippage on each hedge deducted

## Strategy construction, from realised volatility to hedge discipline

| Position | Instrument | Purpose | Allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long straddle, 30 days, at the money | S&P 500 index options | Owns the moves that the hedging trades convert into gains | 4% premium |
| Short out-of-the-money options, 10 delta | S&P 500 index options | Offsets part of the premium cost | 1% margin |
| Delta hedge | E-mini S&P 500 futures | Removes market direction so gains come from movement | 10% margin |
| Treasury bills | US Treasury bills | Provides collateral and earns the cash rate | 85% |

- Entry: Implied volatility at least 1 point below the forecast of realised volatility
- Position size: Premium capped at 4% of capital, scaled to a 7% annualised volatility target
- Exit: Options closed 10 days before expiry, or when time decay exceeds hedging gains
- Stop-loss: All positions closed if losses reach 2% of capital in a month

### Today’s market watch

The gap between implied volatility and recent daily index moves is the main indicator for this strategy, because it shows whether options are cheap compared with the market's movement. Scheduled central bank decisions, trade announcements and geopolitical deadlines matter because they can produce large daily moves in either direction. Rehyn also watches bid-ask spreads on index options, since wider spreads raise hedging costs.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Clusters of large daily moves**: Clusters of large daily moves favour the strategy because each hedge locks in a gain as the index swings back and forth. In March 2020 the S&P 500 moved by more than 5% on several days, far above the moves priced into options earlier in the year.
- **Policy surprises that reprice risk**: Unexpected policy decisions favour the strategy because they produce abrupt moves that option prices did not anticipate. The April 2025 tariff announcement was followed by a fall of about 6% in one day and a rise of about 9.5% in another.
- **Cheap options before a turning point**: Low implied volatility favours the strategy because options then cost little compared with the moves that follow. In February 2018 the VIX had closed near 11 in late January, and the index then fell about 4% on 5 February.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Prolonged calm in index markets**: Long periods of low realised volatility hurt the strategy because the premium paid decays each day while hedging produces little gain. The largest one-day fall in the S&P 500 in 2017 was about 1.8%, so options cost more than the hedging trades returned.
- **Spikes that reverse within days**: Sharp spikes that reverse quickly hurt the strategy because gains from the first large move are given back when the market recovers and the options lose value. After the Brexit vote in June 2016, the S&P 500 recovered its losses within about three weeks.
- **Wider spreads in disorderly markets**: Wider spreads in disorderly markets hurt the strategy because each hedge costs more and prices can gap between trades. Rehyn estimates that transaction costs rose several times in March 2020, which reduced the gain from a very large move.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Long Volatility Returns?

- **Variance risk premium harvesting** (`s1`): Sells the index options this strategy buys. Bears crash risk, losing when volatility rises sharply. *Takes the opposite side*
- **Earnings long straddle** (`s2`): Buys single-stock options before results. Bears premium decay, losing when announcements move shares less than priced. *Long volatility around company events*
- **Long volatility hedging** (`d4`): Holds VIX futures for protection. Bears roll cost, paying steadily in calm markets and gaining in sell-offs. *Simpler hedge without hedging trades*
