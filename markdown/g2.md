# AI supply chain thematic equity

`g2` · Growth · Equities

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: AI Infrastructure Investment Cycle

The investment case rests on sustained growth in computing demand, which requires repeated investment in semiconductors, memory, networks, power, cooling and data centres. The opportunity persists because the largest cloud platforms compete on computing capacity, while US, European and Asian industrial policies support domestic supply chains and strategic control over advanced technology.

The main risk is that supplier earnings depend on concentrated capital spending by a small number of hyperscalers, so weaker guidance can reduce orders across the supply chain. Valuation risk compounds this exposure because expected growth is often priced several years ahead, making the basket sensitive to interest rates and evidence that customers are using capacity more efficiently. The indicative profile of 19.0% annual return, 31.0% volatility, a 55.0% worst fall and 0.1 return skew shows that strong long-term growth comes with large cyclical drawdowns.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the percentage change in the PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index, or SOX, over the episode described. The SOX tracks major listed semiconductor companies and provides a consistent market measure of changing demand, earnings expectations and valuation across the AI supply chain.

**November 2008 · Financial crisis · −69% · strategy lost**  
Credit stress cuts technology investment  
The financial crisis reduced technology orders as customers cut inventories and financing conditions tightened, taking the SOX 69% below its 2007 peak. The strategy lost because semiconductor earnings and valuation multiples fell together.

**May 2019 · Trade policy · −16% · strategy lost**  
Huawei restrictions disrupt chip demand  
US restrictions on Huawei increased the risk of lost sales and excess inventory across global semiconductor suppliers, pushing the SOX down about 16% during May. The strategy lost because China represented a major end market and manufacturing base.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −35% · strategy lost**  
Covid halts orders before cloud demand rises  
The Covid sell-off initially reduced hardware orders and disrupted Asian manufacturing, taking the SOX about 35% below its February peak. The strategy lost because near-term demand and production fell before cloud investment and remote working supported recovery.

**July 2022 · Industrial policy · +16% · strategy gained**  
Chips Act supports domestic capacity  
US approval of semiconductor subsidies improved the outlook for domestic fabrication, equipment and infrastructure investment as the SOX rose about 16% during July. The strategy gained because public support reduced the cost of expanding advanced manufacturing capacity.

**May 2023 · AI demand · +15% · strategy gained**  
Nvidia guidance confirms rapid AI spending  
Nvidia’s revenue guidance showed that hyperscalers were accelerating purchases of AI processors and supporting systems, lifting the SOX about 15% during May. The strategy gained because stronger orders raised earnings expectations across chips, memory, networking and cooling.

**January 2025 · China rivalry · −9.2% · strategy lost**  
DeepSeek challenges expected computing demand  
DeepSeek’s efficient AI model raised concern that advanced systems might require less computing capacity, pushing the SOX down 9.2% on 27 January. The strategy lost because lower expected capital intensity reduced long-term revenue assumptions for infrastructure suppliers.

### Today’s market watch

Hyperscaler capital-spending guidance is the main indicator because it leads orders for processors, memory, networking equipment and power systems. Semiconductor inventories, data-centre power connections and supplier order backlogs show whether spending is reaching the wider supply chain. Earnings reports, export-control decisions and major AI model releases are the key upcoming events.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Hyperscalers raise capital spending**: Higher hyperscaler investment favours the strategy because processor purchases require additional memory, networking, power and cooling capacity. Nvidia’s May 2023 guidance confirmed this mechanism and lifted earnings expectations across the supply chain.
- **Supply constraints support margins**: Tight supply favours the strategy when strong demand allows component producers and equipment suppliers to raise prices and operate factories at high utilisation. The 2020 to 2021 chip shortage supported revenue growth and margins across several semiconductor segments.
- **Industrial policy funds capacity**: Public support favours the strategy because subsidies and tax credits lower the cost of new fabrication plants, power connections and domestic supply chains. US approval of the Chips Act in July 2022 improved the investment outlook for semiconductor equipment and construction suppliers.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Capital spending guidance weakens**: Lower hyperscaler guidance hurts the strategy because suppliers plan capacity against a small number of large customer budgets. The 2018 semiconductor downturn showed how weaker orders and inventory reductions can spread quickly from end customers to equipment and component producers.
- **Export controls restrict sales**: Tighter export controls hurt the strategy by reducing access to major customers and forcing suppliers to redesign products for regulated markets. US restrictions on Huawei in May 2019 lowered expected sales across semiconductor companies with substantial China exposure.
- **Efficiency reduces computing needs**: More efficient AI models hurt the strategy when investors reduce assumptions for processors, power use and data-centre construction. The January 2025 DeepSeek release produced this response because its reported training efficiency challenged expected capital intensity.

## Where Else Can Investors Find AI Growth?

- **Long-term quality growth equity** (`g1`): Owns durable growth across industries and bears valuation risk rather than concentrated AI capital-spending risk. *Broader exposure to long-term growth*
- **Equity momentum investing** (`m2`): Follows equity price trends and bears reversal risk instead of relying on infrastructure demand. *Captures momentum within AI leaders*
- **Inflation-linked infrastructure investing** (`c9`): Owns regulated infrastructure and bears construction, inflation and policy risk while earning from capacity investment. *Extends exposure into power infrastructure*
