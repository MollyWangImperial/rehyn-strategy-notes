# FX trend following

`m4` · Momentum · FX

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Persistent Currency Trends

The investment case rests on currency trends continuing for weeks or months, because exchange rates follow interest rate differentials that central banks change slowly and in steps. The trends persist because policy divergence takes time to appear in capital flows and in the hedging decisions of companies and funds, while geopolitical and trade policy shifts add further directional moves.

The main risk is a sudden reversal after a trend is established, usually when a central bank changes course or investors close positions together. Such reversals can erase several weeks of gains within days, as the rise of the yen in August 2024 showed. The strategy also earns little in periods when central banks move together, because few currencies then trend.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the main currency pair named in the text, over the period described. Currency trend following earns from sustained moves in exchange rates, so a larger move offers more to follow.

**November 2012 · Yen policy · +31% · strategy gained**  
Abenomics weakens the yen for six months  
Between mid-November 2012 and May 2013, USD/JPY rose about 31% as the incoming Japanese government and the Bank of Japan committed to large-scale monetary easing. The strategy gained because the yen fell in a steady trend with few reversals.

**May 2014 · Policy divergence · −25% · strategy gained**  
Diverging central banks lift the dollar  
From May 2014 to March 2015, EUR/USD fell about 25% as the ECB prepared bond purchases while the Fed ended its own and signalled rate rises. The strategy gained because the widening policy gap produced a steady dollar trend.

**June 2016 · Brexit · −8% · strategy mixed**  
Brexit vote sends sterling sharply lower  
GBP/USD fell about 8% on 24 June 2016 after the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union. Results were mixed because short sterling positions gained, but sharp reversals in other currencies reduced the gain.

**March 2022 · Rate divergence · +32% · strategy gained**  
Fed rate rises widen the gap with Japan  
Between March and October 2022, USD/JPY rose about 32% as the Fed raised rates quickly while the Bank of Japan kept its cap on bond yields. The strategy gained because the dollar trend stayed in one direction for most of the period.

**August 2024 · Carry trade · −12.5% · strategy lost**  
Bank of Japan rate rise reverses the yen  
USD/JPY fell about 12.5% between 3 July and 5 August 2024 after a Bank of Japan rate rise and weak US jobs data. The strategy lost because its long dollar positions against the yen reversed within days.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +14% · strategy lost**  
US tariffs reverse the dollar trend  
Between mid-January and 21 April 2025, EUR/USD rose about 14% as US tariffs raised doubts about American growth and weakened the dollar. The strategy lost because dollar positions built during the previous year reversed.

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn watches the gap between policy interest rates in the major economies and the direction of rate expectations, because these gaps drive most sustained currency trends. Changes in currency implied volatility also matter, since falling volatility supports longer trends and rising volatility signals a higher risk of reversal. Central bank meetings, intervention warnings from finance ministries and trade policy announcements are the events most likely to turn a trend.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Diverging central bank policy**: Policy divergence favours the strategy because interest rate differentials attract capital steadily towards the higher-yielding currency. In 2014 and 2015, the ECB's bond purchases and expected Fed rate rises pushed EUR/USD down by about 25%.
- **Firm central bank commitments**: A central bank that holds to a stated policy for months favours the strategy because markets keep adjusting positions in the same direction. The Bank of Japan's cap on bond yields in 2022 supported a rise of about 32% in USD/JPY between March and October.
- **Persistent commodity price trends**: Long-running commodity price moves favour the strategy because the currencies of producing countries follow them for months. As oil prices fell from mid-2014 to early 2016, USD/CAD rose from about 1.06 to above 1.45.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Policy turns that reverse trends**: A change of direction by a central bank hurts the strategy because positions built over months reverse within days. In August 2024, a Bank of Japan rate rise pushed USD/JPY down about 12.5% in five weeks.
- **Government intervention in currencies**: Intervention by governments hurts the strategy because it caps a trend that the economic backdrop still supports and triggers sharp, brief reversals. In October 2022, Japan's Ministry of Finance bought yen and USD/JPY fell by several yen within hours.
- **Range-bound markets with aligned policy**: Periods in which major central banks move together hurt the strategy because currency pairs trade sideways and trend signals repeatedly reverse. In 2019, EUR/USD stayed between about 1.09 and 1.15 for the full year.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Currency Premiums?

- **G10 FX carry trade** (`c1`): Earns the interest rate gap between currencies. Bears crash risk, losing when risk appetite falls. *Paid for rate gaps, not price trends*
- **FX value investing** (`v6`): Buys currencies priced below fair value. Bears drift risk, losing while undervalued currencies keep falling. *Slow valuation signal, opposite to trends*
- **Cross-asset trend following** (`m1`): Follows trends across equities, bonds, currencies and commodities. Bears reversal risk, losing in range-bound markets. *Same signal, spread across asset classes*
