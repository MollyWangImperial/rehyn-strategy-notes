# Stablecoin yield lending

`i9` · Income · Crypto

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Lending Dollars to Leveraged Traders

The investment case rests on the demand of traders and market makers to borrow dollar stablecoins, which they use as leverage and as collateral to buy digital assets, and for which they pay interest above money market rates. The premium persists because many traders cannot borrow dollars from banks against digital assets, and US policy on stablecoins and banking access keeps part of this demand outside regulated lenders.

The main risk is losing deposits when a lending platform fails or when a stablecoin loses its peg, which can happen within days. Losses are rare but large, because the yield earned is small compared with the amount lost if a platform cannot return deposits. A stable return history therefore overstates the safety of the strategy, as the failures of Celsius and of lenders exposed to FTX showed in 2022.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the Bitcoin price at the extreme of the episode: its low in a sell-off or its high in a rally, in US dollars, where k means thousands. Bitcoin prices indicate how much leveraged trading was taking place, because borrowers of stablecoins are mostly traders who use leverage to buy digital assets.

**November 2021 · Bitcoin peak · $69k · strategy gained**  
Bitcoin rally lifts stablecoin borrowing demand  
Bitcoin reached a record high near $69,000 in November 2021, and traders borrowed stablecoins in large amounts to fund leveraged positions. The strategy gained because high borrowing demand lifted lending rates and borrowers repaid their loans at the pegged value.

**June 2022 · Platform failure · $17.6k · strategy lost**  
Celsius freezes withdrawals after the Terra collapse  
Celsius, a large lending platform, froze customer withdrawals on 12 June 2022 after the collapse of the Terra stablecoin, and Bitcoin fell to a low near $17,600. The strategy lost because funds deposited with the platform were locked and later subject to bankruptcy claims.

**November 2022 · Exchange failure · $15.5k · strategy lost**  
FTX bankruptcy spreads losses to lenders  
FTX filed for bankruptcy on 11 November 2022, and Bitcoin fell to a low near $15,500 as lenders with exposure to the exchange halted withdrawals. The strategy lost because platforms that had lent to FTX or its affiliates could not return deposits in full.

**August 2024 · Carry trade · $49k · strategy gained**  
Yen carry unwind tests lending platforms  
A Bank of Japan rate rise and weak US jobs data pushed Bitcoin to a low near $49,000 on 5 August 2024 as leveraged positions were closed. The strategy gained because collateral covered the loans being liquidated, so lenders kept their deposits and earned interest.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · $74.5k · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs lower Bitcoin and trading activity  
The US tariff announcement pushed Bitcoin to a low near $74,500 on 7 April 2025 as investors reduced risk across markets. Results were mixed because borrowing demand fell and lending rates declined, although collateral covered loans and no major platform failed.

**July 2025 · Stablecoin law · $123k · strategy gained**  
US stablecoin law supports lending demand  
The US GENIUS Act, signed in July 2025, set federal rules for stablecoin issuers as Bitcoin reached a high near $123,000. The strategy gained because clearer rules and rising trading activity supported demand for stablecoin borrowing.

- Costs: Estimated network fees and platform withdrawal fees deducted from interest

## Strategy construction, from lending interest to platform risk control

| Position | Instrument | Purpose | Allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over-collateralised lending protocols | USDC on onchain lending protocols | Earns interest from borrowers who post collateral | 40% |
| Regulated custodial lenders | USDC and USDT on regulated platforms | Earns interest with legal protection of deposits | 30% |
| Tokenised Treasury bill funds | Tokenised US Treasury bills | Earns the cash rate with a liquid exit | 20% |
| Cash deposits | US dollar bank deposits | Funds withdrawals and limits platform exposure | 10% |

- Entry: Platform rate at least 1 point above the Treasury bill yield
- Position size: No platform above 25% and no stablecoin above 60%
- Exit: Funds withdrawn if a platform's reserve reports or audits lapse
- Stop-loss: All positions in a stablecoin withdrawn if it trades below 99 cents for 24 hours

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn monitors the lending rate on stablecoins against the Treasury bill yield, the share of loans backed by excess collateral and the discount of each stablecoin to its peg, because these show whether the premium covers the risk. Stablecoin legislation, regulatory action against exchanges and large moves in Bitcoin are the events that matter most. A fall in borrowing demand would reduce the lending rate, while a failure at a large platform would test the diversification of deposits.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Rising demand for leverage**: Rising demand for leverage favours the strategy because traders pay higher interest to borrow stablecoins when digital asset prices are rising. In the 2021 rally, stablecoin lending rates on large platforms rose well above money market rates.
- **Loans backed by excess collateral**: Loans that require excess collateral favour the strategy because the borrower's collateral can be sold to repay the lender if prices fall. In August 2024 large liquidations on onchain lending platforms were covered by collateral, so lenders were repaid in full.
- **Clear regulation and reserve rules**: Clear regulation favours the strategy because issuers hold audited reserves, which lowers the risk that a stablecoin loses its peg. After the GENIUS Act became law in July 2025, issuers in the United States faced reserve and disclosure requirements.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Lending platform failure**: Platform failures hurt the strategy because deposited funds can be frozen or lost when a lender cannot meet withdrawals. In June 2022 Celsius froze withdrawals, and in November 2022 lenders exposed to FTX did the same, leaving depositors with claims in bankruptcy.
- **Stablecoin loses its peg**: A loss of the peg hurts the strategy because the deposited stablecoin is worth less than the dollars lent. In March 2023 USDC traded as low as about 88 cents after Silicon Valley Bank, which held part of its reserves, failed.
- **Falling borrowing demand**: Weak trading hurts the strategy because fewer traders borrow stablecoins, which lowers lending rates towards zero. In 2023 lending rates on large platforms fell below Treasury bill yields as trading volumes declined.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Digital Asset Yield?

- **Treasury bill laddering** (`i1`): Lends to the US government for short periods. Bears reinvestment risk, with no platform or peg risk. *Cash yield without platform risk*
- **Crypto basis trading** (`r2`): Buys spot and sells futures at a premium. Bears exchange and counterparty risk, with returns that depend on leverage demand. *Earns from the same leverage demand*
- **Direct lending** (`i7`): Lends to mid-sized companies at floating rates. Bears credit risk, with infrequent valuations that can hide losses. *Private lending with legal security*
