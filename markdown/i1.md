# Treasury bill laddering

`i1` · Income · Government bonds and rates

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Short-Term Funding Demand

The investment case rests on governments issuing short-dated bills to finance regular spending and central banks maintaining short-term rates to implement monetary policy, allowing a rolling ladder to earn close to the policy rate. The opportunity persists because fiscal funding needs, institutional demand for liquid collateral and geopolitical uncertainty create continuous issuance and trading, while staggered maturities reduce dependence on any single policy decision.

The main risk is that rapid policy tightening raises bill yields before existing tranches mature, producing small mark-to-market losses that can temporarily exceed accrued income. A sustained cutting cycle also reduces returns because each maturing tranche must be reinvested at a lower yield, although existing bills record modest price gains. Inflation is the larger economic risk because it can reduce real income even when nominal returns remain positive and monthly volatility stays low.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the upper bound of the Federal Reserve’s target rate at the end of the episode. This rate anchors Treasury bill yields, so a higher figure generally increases ladder income after maturing tranches have been reinvested.

**December 2008 · Monetary policy · 0.25% · strategy gained**  
Fed cuts rates to the effective floor  
The Federal Reserve cut rates to near zero as the financial crisis reduced credit and economic activity. Existing bills gained as yields fell, but subsequent maturities were reinvested at much lower rates.

**August 2011 · Fiscal policy · 0.25% · strategy mixed**  
Debt ceiling distorts near-term bill prices  
The US debt ceiling dispute and sovereign downgrade increased demand for safe assets while raising concern around selected maturity dates. Results were mixed because most bills gained, but securities exposed to payment delays briefly weakened.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · 0.25% · strategy gained**  
Pandemic flight lifts Treasury bill prices  
The global rush for liquidity pushed investors into Treasury bills as the Federal Reserve cut rates and expanded market support. The ladder gained from falling yields and strong demand, although its future income declined sharply.

**February 2022 · War · 0.25% · strategy mixed**  
Ukraine invasion supports demand for bills  
Russia’s invasion of Ukraine increased demand for liquid government securities, supporting bill prices. Gains remained limited because high inflation kept the Federal Reserve on course to begin raising rates in March.

**September 2022 · Inflation · 3.25% · strategy lost**  
Inflation repricing drives the worst monthly fall  
The Federal Reserve delivered another 75 basis point increase as persistent inflation lifted expected policy rates. Existing bills fell slightly before maturity, producing the ladder’s worst monthly loss, while later reinvestment secured higher income.

**May 2023 · Fiscal policy · 5.25% · strategy mixed**  
Debt ceiling raises yields on specific bills  
The US debt ceiling dispute raised yields on bills maturing near the projected cash exhaustion date. The ladder was mixed because exposed tranches weakened, while other maturities retained safe-haven demand and generated strong carry.

### Today’s market watch

Policy guidance, overnight index swaps and Treasury auction demand indicate the income available when each tranche is reinvested. Inflation releases and central bank meetings matter because unexpected tightening can cause small price losses, while rate cuts reduce future income. Fiscal deadlines also matter when payment concerns affect specific bill maturities.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **High stable policy rates**: High and stable rates favour the strategy because each maturing tranche is reinvested at an attractive yield without material losses on existing bills. The 2023 to 2024 period followed this pattern after the Federal Reserve completed most of its tightening cycle.
- **Gradual rate increases**: Gradual tightening favours the ladder because short maturities limit price declines while frequent reinvestment raises portfolio income. During the measured increases of 2017 and 2018, higher coupon accrual broadly offset the small mark-to-market effect.
- **Flight to government liquidity**: A flight to liquidity favours the strategy because demand for Treasury bills pushes yields lower and raises the value of existing tranches. In March 2020, bills gained as investors sought cash-like government securities and the Federal Reserve cut rates rapidly.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Rapid unexpected tightening**: Rapid tightening hurts the strategy because bill yields rise before accrued income can offset the decline in existing prices. In September 2022, persistent inflation and a 75 basis point Federal Reserve increase produced the backtest’s worst monthly fall.
- **Early sustained rate cuts**: Sustained easing reduces income because each maturing tranche must be reinvested at a lower yield, outweighing the brief price gain on existing bills. After the emergency cuts of 2020, ladder income remained close to zero until policy tightening began in 2022.
- **Debt ceiling maturity distortions**: Debt ceiling disputes hurt exposed tranches because investors demand additional yield on bills that may mature near a payment deadline. In May 2023, yields on selected June maturities rose even as other Treasury bills retained strong safe-haven demand.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Short-Rate Returns?

- **Sovereign bond relative value** (`v4`): Trades pricing differences between sovereign bonds and bears convergence, funding and policy risk rather than simple reinvestment risk. *Adds relative value across government curves*
- **Yield curve butterfly trading** (`r5`): Trades yield-curve shape and bears the risk that policy or issuance causes persistent dislocations. *Uses curve shape rather than carry*
- **Central bank event trading** (`e4`): Trades rate moves around policy decisions and bears event risk from outcomes that differ from market expectations. *Targets the policy events affecting reinvestment*
