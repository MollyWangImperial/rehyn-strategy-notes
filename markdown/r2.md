# Crypto basis trading

`r2` · Mean reversion · Crypto

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Futures Premium Over Spot

The investment case rests on digital asset futures trading at a premium to the spot price when demand for leveraged long exposure is strong, so a buyer of the spot asset who sells the futures contract earns the premium as the two prices converge at expiry. The premium persists because speculators prefer to borrow through futures rather than hold the asset, and arbitrage capital is limited by exchange credit limits and bank funding, while regulatory decisions such as the approval of US spot bitcoin ETFs in January 2024 change who can access leverage.

The main risk is counterparty failure, because the price hedge works only if the exchange or custodian holding the collateral stays solvent and open for withdrawals. In November 2022 the failure of FTX showed that collateral held on a venue can be frozen even when both legs of the trade are correctly hedged. A second risk is that the premium falls to zero in a sell-off, which leaves little return for the capital tied up, while forced liquidation of one leg during a sharp move can cause losses on the other.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the price of bitcoin over the month of the episode. A rise lifts demand for leveraged long positions and widens the futures premium, while a fall narrows it and can trigger forced liquidations.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −25% · strategy lost**  
Covid sell-off forces leveraged exchange liquidations  
Bitcoin fell by more than a third on 12 March 2020 as investors sold to raise cash, and it ended the month 25% lower. The strategy lost because exchanges liquidated leveraged positions and the futures premium disappeared, so margin calls forced closing at a loss.

**December 2020 · Institutional demand · +47% · strategy gained**  
Institutional buying lifts bitcoin to record highs  
Bitcoin rose 47% in December 2020 as institutions and payment companies increased purchases, passing its 2017 high of about $20,000. The strategy gained because demand for leveraged long positions pushed the futures premium well above the cost of cash.

**May 2021 · China ban · −35% · strategy lost**  
China mining ban triggers heavy liquidations  
Bitcoin fell 35% in May 2021 after Chinese authorities moved against mining and crypto services and Tesla suspended bitcoin payments. The strategy lost because liquidations on offshore exchanges cut the futures premium and forced leveraged traders to close positions.

**November 2022 · Exchange failure · −16% · strategy lost**  
FTX collapse exposes counterparty risk  
Bitcoin fell 16% in November 2022 as the exchange FTX filed for bankruptcy and froze customer withdrawals. The strategy lost because collateral held on the exchange became inaccessible, and funds had to close hedges at wider spreads on other venues.

**March 2024 · Spot ETFs · +16% · strategy gained**  
Spot bitcoin ETF inflows widen the futures premium  
Bitcoin rose 16% in March 2024 to a record near $73,000 after US spot bitcoin ETFs, approved in January, drew steady inflows. The strategy gained because demand for leveraged long positions lifted the futures premium to its highest level in about three years.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +14% · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs test the futures premium  
Bitcoin fell below $75,000 on 7 April 2025 after the announcement of US tariffs, then recovered to end the month 14% higher. Results were mixed because the futures premium narrowed during the sell-off and widened again as the price recovered.

- Entry: Annualised premium at least 4 points above the cash rate
- Position size: Set to a 6% annualised volatility target, with at most 30% of capital on one venue
- Exit: Position closed at expiry, or when the premium falls below the cash rate
- Stop-loss: All positions reduced if any venue delays withdrawals

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Strong demand for leveraged exposure**: Rising demand for leveraged long positions favours the strategy because traders pay a higher futures premium to hold exposure. In December 2020 and March 2024, steady buying lifted the premium well above the cash rate.
- **Access through regulated venues**: Wider access through regulated exchanges and ETFs favours the strategy because it brings steady institutional demand and reduces counterparty risk. After US spot ETFs were approved in January 2024, inflows rose and the futures premium stayed wide for about three months.
- **Orderly rallies with stable funding**: Orderly rallies favour the strategy because the gain on the spot position covers margin on the short futures leg, so the trade can run to expiry. In the 2020 rally, positions held to expiry earned the full premium.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Exchange or custodian failure**: A venue failure hurts the strategy because collateral can be frozen or lost even when the price hedge works. In November 2022, FTX froze withdrawals and clients lost access to assets held on the exchange.
- **Forced liquidations in sell-offs**: Sharp sell-offs hurt the strategy because liquidations narrow the premium and margin calls force positions to close before expiry. On 12 March 2020 bitcoin fell by more than a third in a day, and exchanges liquidated large leveraged positions.
- **Stablecoin and funding stress**: A loss of confidence in a stablecoin hurts the strategy because collateral and settlement depend on it, so positions cannot be moved quickly. In March 2023, USDC briefly traded near 88 cents after the failure of Silicon Valley Bank.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Crypto Market Returns?

- **Stablecoin yield lending** (`i9`): Lends dollar stablecoins for interest. Bears platform and peg risk, losing if a borrower or stablecoin fails. *Similar yield, with no futures hedge*
- **Crypto volatility arbitrage** (`s7`): Trades the gap between implied and realised volatility in digital asset options. Bears liquidity risk, losing in sudden price jumps. *Earns from option prices instead of futures*
- **Crypto trend following** (`m6`): Holds digital assets in uptrends and exits in downtrends. Bears whipsaw risk, losing when prices reverse repeatedly. *Earns from price direction this strategy hedges*
