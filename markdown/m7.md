# Fixed income trend following

`m7` · Momentum · Government bonds and rates

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Persistent Interest Rate Cycles

The investment case rests on government bond yields trending in one direction for months, because central banks change policy rates in a sequence of small steps and signal their path in advance. The pattern persists because growth, inflation and borrowing news arrive gradually, so investors adjust their holdings over time, and shifts in trade policy and government borrowing can push yields in one direction for long periods.

The main risk is a sudden change in interest rate expectations that reverses a trend within days, usually after a surprise in inflation, growth or central bank guidance. The strategy then holds positions in the wrong direction and loses the gains of several weeks. It also earns little when yields move sideways, because trend signals reverse repeatedly and each trade incurs costs.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the US 10-year Treasury yield over the period described in the text, in basis points, where 100 basis points equal one percentage point. Bond trend following earns from sustained moves in yields in either direction, so a larger move offers more to follow.

**May 2013 · Taper tantrum · +135bp · strategy mixed**  
Fed taper signal lifts Treasury yields  
Between 2 May and 5 September 2013, the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 135bp after the Fed signalled that it would slow bond purchases. Results were mixed because the first rise reversed long positions at a loss, while the later trend rewarded short positions.

**August 2019 · Trade war · −55bp · strategy gained**  
Tariff escalation drives a bond rally  
The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 55bp between July and late August 2019 as higher US tariffs on Chinese goods raised growth concerns. The strategy gained because it held long bond positions while yields fell steadily.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −130bp · strategy mixed**  
Covid sends yields to record lows then reverses  
The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 130bp from mid-January to 9 March 2020 as Covid spread, then rose about 65bp in nine days as investors sold Treasuries for cash. Results were mixed because long bond positions gained in the fall but lost in the reversal.

**January 2022 · Rate rises · +260bp · strategy gained**  
Fed tightening starts the steepest bond sell-off  
Between January and October 2022, the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 260bp as the Fed raised rates to control inflation. The strategy gained because short bond positions rewarded the steady rise in yields.

**March 2023 · Bank failure · −50bp · strategy lost**  
Silicon Valley Bank failure reverses bond trends  
The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 50bp between 8 and 15 March 2023 after the failure of Silicon Valley Bank, as investors expected the Fed to pause. The strategy lost because short positions built during the earlier rise in yields reversed within days.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +48bp · strategy lost**  
US tariffs lift Treasury yields sharply  
The 10-year Treasury yield rose about 48bp in the week to 11 April 2025, the largest weekly rise since 2001, as US tariffs unsettled bond investors. The strategy lost because long positions built during the earlier decline in yields reversed within days.

- Position size: Set to a 7% annualised volatility target, with each market at most 25% of risk
- Exit: Position closed when the 1-month trend turns against the 6-month trend
- Stop-loss: Market position closed after a loss of 1.5% of portfolio value

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn watches the direction of policy rate expectations in the major economies and the pace of change in inflation, because these set the length of yield trends. Rising interest rate volatility suggests a higher risk of reversal. Central bank meetings, inflation releases and government borrowing announcements are the events most likely to start or end a trend.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Clear central bank rate cycles**: Rate cycles that central banks signal in advance favour the strategy because yields keep moving in one direction for many months. In 2022, successive Fed rate rises lifted the 10-year Treasury yield by about 260bp, and the strategy gained from short positions.
- **Flight to safety in slowdowns**: Slowing growth favours the strategy because investors buy government bonds and yields fall in a steady trend. During the 2019 trade war, the 10-year Treasury yield fell about 55bp, and long positions gained.
- **Persistent inflation or heavy borrowing**: Persistent inflation or heavier government borrowing favours the strategy because yields rise over several quarters as investors ask for higher compensation. The UK 10-year gilt yield rose from about 1% at the end of 2021 to above 4.5% in 2023.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Policy pivots after long trends**: A sudden shift in central bank guidance hurts the strategy when it reverses a trend that has lasted for months. In March 2023, the failure of Silicon Valley Bank cut the 10-year Treasury yield by about 50bp in a week and reversed short positions.
- **Forced selling that reverses trends**: Forced selling of government bonds hurts the strategy when it reverses long positions within days. In mid-March 2020, the 10-year yield rose about 65bp in nine days as investors sold Treasuries to raise cash, after a fall of about 130bp.
- **Range-bound yields without direction**: Periods when yields move sideways hurt the strategy because trend signals reverse repeatedly and trading costs accumulate. In 2017, the 10-year Treasury yield stayed between about 2.0% and 2.6% for the full year.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Interest Rate Premiums?

- **Long-duration Treasury hedge** (`d6`): Holds long-dated Treasuries permanently. Bears inflation risk, losing when yields rise and bonds fall with equities. *Static duration, without a trend filter*
- **Yield curve butterfly trading** (`r5`): Trades kinks in the yield curve. Bears curve risk, losing when a kink keeps widening. *Neutral to the level of rates*
- **Central bank event trading** (`e4`): Positions in rates around central bank meetings. Bears event risk, losing when decisions surprise. *Short-term events, not sustained trends*
