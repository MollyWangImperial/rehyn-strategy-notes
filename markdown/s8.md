# Oil event volatility trading

`s8` · Short term volatility · Commodities

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Underpriced Oil Supply Disruption Risk

The investment case rests on oil options often pricing smaller price moves than supply disruptions can cause, because most geopolitical threats pass without reducing the oil that reaches the market. The pricing gap persists because sellers of oil options earn steady income in quiet periods, so option prices rarely allow fully for the rare disruption that moves oil by 10% or more in a day.

The main risk is a long run of small losses when threats pass without a disruption and the options expire worthless. Implied volatility often rises before an event, so positions bought after the market has already priced the risk can lose even when the event occurs. Rehyn estimates that returns depend on a small number of large winning events, which makes results uneven and difficult to forecast.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the Brent crude oil price on the first trading day after the event. Brent typically moves by about 2% in a day, so a move of 7% or more indicates a significant disruption in the market.

**September 2019 · Geopolitics · +15% · strategy gained**  
Attack on Saudi facilities cuts supply  
Drone attacks on Saudi oil facilities on 14 September 2019 removed about half of the country's output, and Brent rose 15% on 16 September, its largest one-day rise since 1988. The strategy gained because the move far exceeded the range priced into options.

**January 2020 · Geopolitics · +3.5% · strategy mixed**  
US strike on Iranian general lifts Brent briefly  
A US strike killed Iran's General Soleimani on 3 January 2020, and Brent rose about 3.5% that day before returning to its earlier level within a week. Results were mixed because early option gains were offset by lower implied volatility afterwards.

**March 2020 · Price war · −24% · strategy gained**  
Saudi-Russia price war cuts Brent by a quarter  
After OPEC and Russia failed to agree output cuts, Saudi Arabia cut prices and raised production, and Brent fell about 24% on 9 March 2020. The strategy gained because the fall was far larger than the move implied by option prices.

**October 2023 · Middle East · +4.2% · strategy lost**  
Hamas attack on Israel lifts Brent briefly  
Hamas's attack on Israel on 7 October 2023 raised fears of a wider conflict, and Brent rose about 4.2% on 9 October. The strategy lost because oil supply was not disrupted and prices returned to their earlier level within about a month.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · −6.4% · strategy mixed**  
Tariffs and OPEC+ output rise cut Brent  
US tariff announcements and a decision by OPEC+ to raise output by more than expected cut Brent about 6.4% on 3 April 2025. Results were mixed because the fall generated option gains but implied volatility had already risen before the announcements.

**June 2025 · Middle East · +7% · strategy gained**  
Israeli strikes on Iran lift oil prices  
Israel's strikes on Iran on 13 June 2025 raised the risk to supply through the Strait of Hormuz, and Brent rose about 7% that day. The strategy gained because the move exceeded the range implied by options, although prices fell back after a ceasefire.

## Quick backtest across 16 years of market shocks

Return 9.9% · volatility 12.6% · Sharpe 0.51 · worst fall −18% (November 2023)

- Period: January 2010 to December 2025; 192 monthly observations
- Instrument: Brent and WTI crude oil options; one to three months to expiry; bought ahead of scheduled and emerging supply events
- Rules: Strangles and call spreads bought when implied volatility is below its average level around comparable supply events; sized by premium; closed after the event window or at a profit target
- Costs: Estimated option spreads, futures commissions and slippage deducted at each entry and exit

## Strategy construction, from event convexity to limited premium loss

| Position | Instrument | Purpose | Allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long strangle, 5% out of the money | Brent crude oil options | Gains from large price moves in either direction | 3% premium |
| Long call spread, 10% out of the money | WTI crude oil options | Gains from a supply disruption at a lower cost | 1% premium |
| Delta hedge | Brent crude oil futures | Adjusts exposure after a large price move | 5% margin |
| Treasury bills | US Treasury bills | Provides collateral and earns the cash rate | 91% |

- Entry: Implied volatility at least 20% below its average level around comparable supply events
- Position size: Premium capped at 3% of capital, with no more than two events held at once
- Exit: Closed five days after the event, or when the gain reaches 100% of premium paid
- Stop-loss: No price stop; loss limited to the premium paid; options closed 10 days before expiry if no event occurs

### Today’s market watch

The gap between implied oil volatility and the price moves that followed past supply events is the main indicator for this strategy now. OPEC+ meetings, shipping disruptions in the Gulf and sanctions decisions matter because they can change expected supply within days. Rehyn also watches how much of each threat is already priced into the oil futures curve.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Supply threats priced too lightly**: Options that price a threat too lightly favour the strategy because a genuine disruption then moves oil by much more than implied. In September 2019 Brent rose 15% in a day after the attack on Saudi facilities, a move the options market had not priced.
- **Producer policy surprises**: Unexpected OPEC+ decisions favour the strategy because they change expected supply abruptly and leave option prices behind. In March 2020 the failure of OPEC and Russia to agree output cuts led to a fall of about 24% in Brent on 9 March.
- **Cheap options before known risks**: Low implied volatility before a known risk favours the strategy because the premium paid is small compared with a possible move. Brent rose about 7% on 13 June 2025, the first day of Israel's strikes on Iran, which exceeded the range implied by options.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Threats that pass without disruption**: Threats that do not reduce supply hurt the strategy because oil prices fall back and the options lose value. After the Hamas attack on Israel in October 2023, Brent rose about 4.2% on 9 October but returned to its earlier level within about a month.
- **Option prices that already include risk**: Expensive options hurt the strategy because the price must move further to cover the higher premium. Implied volatility on oil options was elevated in April 2025, so the fall in Brent of about 6.4% on 3 April produced a smaller gain than the move alone suggests.
- **Spare capacity limits price effects**: Ample spare capacity among producers hurts the strategy because OPEC+ and other suppliers can offset a disruption within weeks. Rehyn estimates that this reduced the lasting price effect of the January 2020 US strike on Iran's General Soleimani, when Brent returned to its earlier level within a week.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Commodity Event Returns?

- **Supply shock event trading** (`e6`): Takes directional positions on supply shocks rather than buying volatility. Bears event timing risk, losing when disruptions do not occur. *Directional version of the same view*
- **Commodity trend following** (`m5`): Follows persistent commodity price trends. Bears reversals, losing when prices change direction after a disruption. *Gains after the move, not before*
- **Commodity roll yield carry** (`c3`): Earns the price gap between near and later oil futures. Bears reversals when supply tightness eases. *Benefits from tight supply without options*
