# Commodity roll yield carry

`c3` · Macro and carry · Commodities

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Capturing Commodity Futures Roll Yield

The investment case rests on the tendency of futures prices in backwardated commodity markets to rise towards higher spot prices as contracts approach delivery, so holding them earns a roll yield even when spot prices are unchanged. The return persists because consumers pay a premium for immediate supply when inventories are low, and sanctions, export restrictions and underinvestment in production keep inventories tight, while few investors are willing to take on the cost and risk of storing physical commodities.

The main risk is that prices fall by more than the roll yield earns, so a downturn in demand can produce large losses. Backwardated markets are usually those in which supply is already disrupted, so the end of a disruption or a drop in demand can reverse prices and remove the roll yield at the same time. A high carry return therefore gives little protection in the sharp falls that follow a supply shock, as in March 2020 and December 2022.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the Brent crude oil settlement price, in US dollars per barrel, at the peak or trough of the episode. Brent is the main global benchmark for oil, so it shows how strongly energy markets moved while the strategy held its positions.

**January 2016 · Oil oversupply · 28 · strategy gained**  
Oil oversupply pushes futures into contango  
Rising US shale output and OPEC's refusal to cut production pushed Brent to about 28 dollars in January 2016, the lowest since 2003. The strategy gained because it was short in contango markets, where futures fall towards spot prices as contracts roll.

**September 2019 · Supply attack · 69 · strategy mixed**  
Attack on Saudi oil facilities lifts Brent  
The 14 September 2019 attack on Saudi Arabia's Abqaiq facility cut about 5% of global supply and lifted Brent by 15% in one day to 69 dollars. Results were mixed because backwardation widened briefly, but prices fell back within two weeks as output returned.

**March 2020 · Price war · 34 · strategy lost**  
OPEC talks fail as Covid cuts oil demand  
After OPEC and Russia failed to agree production cuts in March 2020, Saudi Arabia announced higher output and Brent fell 24% in one day to 34 dollars. The strategy lost because backwardated energy contracts fell sharply and curves moved into contango.

**October 2021 · Energy shortage · 86 · strategy gained**  
Energy shortages lift oil and gas prices  
Low inventories and a rebound in demand after lockdowns lifted Brent to about 86 dollars in October 2021, its highest in three years. The strategy gained because prices in backwardated markets moved up towards the higher spot price.

**March 2022 · War · 128 · strategy gained**  
Russian invasion lifts Brent to a 14-year high  
Russia's invasion of Ukraine and fears of lost energy exports lifted Brent to a settlement of 128 dollars on 8 March 2022, the highest since 2008. The strategy gained because prices rose across markets that were already in steep backwardation.

**December 2022 · Demand fears · 77 · strategy lost**  
Recession fears reverse the oil price spike  
Recession fears and weaker Chinese demand pulled Brent down to about 77 dollars in December 2022, below its level at the start of the year. The strategy lost because the fall in prices outweighed the roll yield earned.

- Exit: Position closed when the curve slope falls below 2%, or at the next monthly roll
- Stop-loss: A position is closed if it loses 8% from its entry price

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Tight inventories and supply disruption**: Low inventories favour the strategy because buyers pay more for immediate delivery, which widens backwardation and raises the roll yield. After Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, curves in oil and European gas moved into steep backwardation and the strategy earned a large roll return.
- **Contango from oversupply**: Oversupply favours the strategy because the short leg earns the roll-down as futures prices fall towards spot prices. Crude oil curves stayed in contango while Brent fell from about 60 dollars in mid-2015 to about 28 dollars in January 2016, so short positions gained.
- **Steady demand growth**: Steady economic growth favours the strategy because consumption keeps inventories low and curves in backwardation. In 2021, the recovery from Covid lockdowns lifted demand for energy and metals above supply, and Brent rose by about 50% over the year.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Sudden fall in demand**: A sudden fall in demand hurts the strategy because prices fall by more than the roll yield earned and curves flatten or move into contango. In March 2020, Brent fell by about 55% in a month as Covid-19 spread and a price war began.
- **End of a supply disruption**: The end of a disruption hurts the strategy because prices fall back to their earlier level and the curve flattens, so the roll yield disappears. After the Abqaiq attack in September 2019, Brent gave up most of its 15% one-day rise within two weeks as Saudi output was restored.
- **Release of strategic stocks**: Policy releases of stored supply hurt the strategy because they reduce tightness and flatten backwardation. In 2022, the US announced a record release of 180 million barrels from its Strategic Petroleum Reserve, and the Brent curve spread narrowed through the second half of the year.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Commodity Carry?

- **Commodity trend following** (`m5`): Follows price trends in energy, metals and agriculture. Bears reversals, losing when prices change direction. *Trends often accompany steep backwardation*
- **Commodity spread trading** (`r6`): Trades spreads between related contracts. Bears spread widening, losing when relationships between contracts break down. *Targets spreads, not outright price direction*
- **Supply shock event trading** (`e6`): Trades supply disruptions using past episodes. Bears timing risk, losing when supply responds faster than expected. *Gains from shocks that steepen backwardation*
