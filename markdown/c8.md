# Autocallable note laddering

`c8` · Macro and carry · Private and structured

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Selling Equity Downside Risk

The investment case rests on the coupon that autocallable notes pay in return for the investor accepting losses if an equity index falls below a barrier, a coupon that has usually exceeded the losses investors have suffered in practice. The premium persists because long-dated index options are consistently priced above the losses they pay out, as institutional investors buy protection against falls regardless of price, and geopolitical and policy uncertainty keeps that demand high.

The main risk is a loss of principal if an index closes below its barrier on the final date, because the loss is then the full fall in the index from its starting level. Several notes can reach their barriers at the same time because they refer to the same markets, so a single market fall affects the whole ladder. A high coupon therefore overstates the safety of the return, as it does not compensate for the rare periods when equity indices fall by more than 30%.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the S&P 500 over the episode, measured from the last high to the low in a fall and from the low to the high in a rally. Autocallable notes depend on where the index closes on set observation dates, so the figure shows how close the index came to the level that triggers early redemption or a loss of principal.

**August 2011 · US downgrade · −17% · strategy mixed**  
US downgrade delays early redemption of notes  
The US credit rating downgrade and the euro area debt crisis cut the S&P 500 by 17% in August 2011, so many notes missed early redemption dates. Results were mixed because the barriers held, but coupons were deferred and note prices fell.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −34% · strategy lost**  
Covid sell-off pushes indices close to barriers  
The S&P 500 fell 34% from 19 February to 23 March 2020, and the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 38%, bringing both close to the barriers that protect note principal. The strategy lost because note prices fell sharply and early redemptions stopped.

**November 2020 · Vaccine rally · +11% · strategy gained**  
Vaccine news lifts shares and triggers redemptions  
Positive Covid-19 vaccine results and the end of US election uncertainty lifted the S&P 500 by 11% in November 2020. The strategy gained because the index closed above the redemption level on many observation dates, so notes were repaid early with coupons.

**June 2022 · Inflation · −24% · strategy lost**  
Rate rises cut note prices and delay redemption  
The S&P 500 fell 24% from January to 16 June 2022 as the Federal Reserve raised rates and the war in Ukraine lifted energy prices. The strategy lost because the notes moved closer to their barriers and higher volatility reduced their prices.

**November 2023 · Rate cut hopes · +9% · strategy gained**  
Rate cut hopes lift shares and redemptions  
Falling inflation and signs that the Federal Reserve had stopped raising rates lifted the S&P 500 by 9% in November 2023. The strategy gained because many notes passed their redemption levels and were repaid with coupons.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · −19% · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs push indices towards note barriers  
US tariff announcements cut the S&P 500 by 19% from 19 February to 8 April 2025, and the index recovered most of the fall by late May. Results were mixed because note prices fell sharply, but barriers held and redemptions resumed.

- Entry: Barrier at 65% or lower and an annual coupon of at least 7%
- Position size: No more than 20% of capital in one note and 70% in all notes
- Exit: Notes held until early redemption or maturity, whichever comes first
- Stop-loss: New notes paused if any index is more than 15% below its 12-month high

### Today’s market watch

The level of each index relative to its note barrier, implied volatility and the coupon offered on new notes are the main indicators for this strategy. A rise in correlation between the S&P 500, the Euro Stoxx 50 and the Nikkei 225 matters because it reduces the benefit of holding notes on different markets.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Steady equity market gains**: Steady index gains favour the strategy because notes are redeemed early with their coupons and the capital can be reinvested in new notes. From 2012 to 2019, the S&P 500 recorded a positive total return in seven of eight years.
- **High implied volatility at purchase**: High implied volatility favours the strategy because it raises the coupon that issuers can pay on new notes for the same barrier. In March 2020, the VIX closed above 50 on most days between 12 March and 2 April, so notes bought then carried much higher coupons.
- **Quick market recoveries**: Fast recoveries favour the strategy because index levels return above the redemption level before the final observation date. The S&P 500 regained its February 2020 high by August 2020, and notes with later observation dates were redeemed with their coupons.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Index falls below the barrier**: A fall below the barrier hurts the strategy because the investor then bears the full loss in the index, not just the amount beyond the barrier. In March 2020, the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 38% in four weeks, close to the barriers of 60% to 65% set on many notes.
- **Rising correlation between indices**: A rise in correlation hurts the strategy because falls in different indices happen together, so the loss is not diversified across the ladder. In March 2020, the S&P 500, Euro Stoxx 50 and Nikkei 225 each fell by more than 25% within five weeks.
- **Rising implied volatility**: A rise in implied volatility hurts the strategy because the options sold inside each note increase in price, so notes lose value before maturity even if barriers are not breached. In 2022 the S&P 500 fell at most 25% from its high, so barriers at 65% held, yet note prices fell as volatility rose.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Structured Returns?

- **Variance risk premium harvesting** (`s1`): Sells short-dated index options directly. Bears volatility spikes, losing in sharp sell-offs. *Similar premium, shorter and more liquid*
- **Covered call writing** (`i5`): Sells call options against shares. Bears equity falls and capped gains, losing when stocks decline. *Keeps equity exposure, with no barrier*
- **Catastrophe bond investing** (`e7`): Earns insurance premiums from catastrophe risk. Bears disaster losses, largely unrelated to financial markets. *Structured income from a different risk*
