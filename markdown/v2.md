# Quality at a reasonable price

`v2` · Value · Equities

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Mispricing Durable Corporate Quality

The investment case rests on buying companies with high returns on capital and strong balance sheets when short-term concerns push their shares below estimated intrinsic value. The opportunity persists because investors often overreact to earnings setbacks and policy or geopolitical risks, while mandates tied to benchmarks and quarterly performance discourage the patience needed to wait for valuation gaps to close.

The main risk is that an apparently cheap company suffers a lasting decline in earnings power, turning a valuation discount into a value trap. Losses can also be substantial during recessions because even financially strong companies face weaker demand, lower margins and falling market valuations. The estimated -0.3 return skew and -31.0% worst fall show that valuation discipline reduces risk but does not prevent large equity drawdowns.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Temporary earnings weakness reverses**: A short-lived earnings setback favours the strategy when the share price falls more than intrinsic value because investors focus on near-term guidance. High-quality industrial companies followed this pattern after the 2015 China slowdown, recovering as orders and margins stabilised.
- **Market recoveries broaden**: A broader recovery favours the strategy because discounted companies begin to participate after investors initially concentrate on defensive or high-growth shares. The November 2020 vaccine rally produced this pattern as financial, industrial and consumer companies joined the equity advance.
- **Capital discipline improves returns**: Better capital allocation favours the strategy because lower debt, fewer acquisitions and disciplined investment can raise free cash flow without rapid revenue growth. Large US companies demonstrated this after the global financial crisis as balance sheets strengthened and share repurchases increased.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Recession cuts intrinsic value**: A deep recession hurts the strategy because lower demand and weaker margins reduce intrinsic value rather than merely depressing the share price. In March 2020, abrupt economic closures caused earnings estimates to fall across otherwise strong companies.
- **Inflation compresses profit margins**: Persistent inflation hurts the strategy when companies cannot pass higher wages, energy and input costs to customers. The 2022 inflation shock reduced margins and raised discount rates, so reasonable valuations provided only partial protection.
- **Quality becomes a value trap**: Structural decline hurts the strategy when historical returns on capital overstate a company’s future earnings power. Traditional media and retail companies provided repeated examples after 2010 as digital competition weakened pricing power and reduced asset values.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Equity Quality Returns?

- **Long-term quality growth equity** (`g1`): Owns durable growers without a strict valuation discount, so it bears more valuation and interest-rate risk. *Similar quality, less valuation discipline*
- **Equity momentum investing** (`m2`): Follows relative price strength rather than intrinsic value, bearing reversal risk when market leadership changes. *Adds a price trend signal*
- **Dividend quality investing** (`i4`): Emphasises dependable dividends, bearing payout and sector concentration risk rather than relying mainly on valuation gains. *Quality exposure with more income*
