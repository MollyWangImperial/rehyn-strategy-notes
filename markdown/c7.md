# Global macro futures overlay

`c7` · Macro and carry · Futures

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Capturing Macro Policy Repricing

The investment case rests on the tendency of markets to adjust slowly to changes in growth, inflation and central bank policy, which allows directional positions in equity index, bond and currency futures to earn a return as prices adjust. The opportunity persists because central banks change policy gradually, investors with fixed mandates adjust their holdings slowly, and geopolitical events and trade policy change the outlook for inflation and growth faster than most portfolios can react.

The main risk is a wrong view on the economy or policy, because the strategy takes directional positions that do not offset each other. Losses can build quickly when markets reverse after a policy announcement or a geopolitical event, as the same view often appears in several futures markets at once. Diversifying across equity, bond and currency futures therefore reduces risk only when the markets respond to different drivers, and it gives little protection when they move together.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the highest closing level of the VIX during the episode. The VIX measures the volatility that S&P 500 options price in for the next 30 days: it has averaged about 19 over the long run, while readings above 30 signal market stress.

**August 2011 · US downgrade · 48 · strategy gained**  
US downgrade and euro crisis lift the VIX  
The August 2011 downgrade of the US credit rating and the euro area debt crisis lifted the VIX to a 48 close. The strategy gained because long Treasury futures rose as the 10-year yield fell below 2.4%.

**August 2015 · China devaluation · 41 · strategy lost**  
China devalues the yuan and markets reverse  
China's devaluation of the yuan on 11 August 2015 triggered a global sell-off that lifted the VIX to a 41 close on 24 August. The strategy lost because long equity index futures fell sharply in one session before position limits could reduce exposure.

**December 2018 · Fed tightening · 36 · strategy lost**  
Fed tightening and trade tension hit equities  
The Federal Reserve's December 2018 rate rise and trade tension took the S&P 500 down 20% from its September high and the VIX to a 36 close. The strategy lost because long equity positions fell and positions for higher yields reversed.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · 83 · strategy mixed**  
Covid sell-off becomes a global rush for cash  
The March 2020 sell-off lifted the VIX to an 83 close on 16 March as investors sold equities, bonds and commodities to raise cash. Results were mixed because short equity positions gained early, while falls in Treasury and oil futures hurt other positions.

**March 2022 · War · 36 · strategy gained**  
Ukraine invasion lifts inflation and bond yields  
Russia's invasion of Ukraine raised energy prices and inflation expectations, and the VIX reached a 36 close on 7 March 2022 as the Federal Reserve prepared its first rate rise. The strategy gained on short Treasury futures and long energy futures.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · 52 · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs lift volatility to a five-year high  
The April 2025 announcement of broad US tariffs lifted the VIX to a 52 close on 8 April, while the dollar fell and long-term Treasury yields rose. Results were mixed because short equity positions gained but long Treasury and dollar positions lost.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Clear policy divergence**: Different policy paths between central banks favour the strategy because they move interest rates and currencies in consistent directions. In 2022 the Federal Reserve raised rates by 425 basis points while the Bank of Japan kept its rate at minus 0.1%, and USD/JPY rose by about 14%.
- **Persistent inflation surprises**: Inflation above expectations favours the strategy because it lifts bond yields and commodity prices in the same direction for months. US consumer price inflation rose from 1.4% in January 2021 to 9.1% in June 2022, and the 10-year yield rose from about 1% to above 3%.
- **Risk aversion with falling yields**: Falling equity prices combined with falling yields favour the strategy because short equity and long bond positions gain together. In August 2011, the S&P 500 fell about 17% in three weeks while the 10-year Treasury yield fell from 3.0% to 2.3%.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Abrupt reversal of policy expectations**: A sudden change in policy expectations hurts the strategy because positions built over weeks lose value within days. In December 2018, the 10-year Treasury yield fell from 3.2% in November to 2.7% as the Fed signalled a pause, reversing positions for higher yields.
- **Sharp one-day reversals**: A one-day reversal hurts the strategy because futures positions carry leverage and stop orders may not be filled at the intended price. On 24 August 2015, the S&P 500 fell 3.9% after falling more than 5% earlier in the session.
- **Rising correlation between markets**: A rise in correlation hurts the strategy because positions in different markets stop offsetting one another. Between 9 and 18 March 2020, US equity, oil and Treasury futures all fell on several days, so the diversification across markets disappeared.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Macro Returns?

- **Cross-asset trend following** (`m1`): Follows price trends in futures without forming economic views. Bears reversals, losing when trends break down. *Trades price trends instead of forecasts*
- **Central bank event trading** (`e4`): Positions in rates only around central bank meetings. Bears announcement risk, losing when decisions surprise the market. *Narrower focus on policy events*
- **Risk parity allocation** (`c5`): Balances risk across equities, bonds and commodities, with leverage. Bears inflation shocks, losing when stocks and bonds fall together. *Holds assets without directional views*
