# Thematic growth ETF allocation

`g3` · Growth · ETFs and funds

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Diversified Thematic Growth

The investment case rests on structural spending across automation, clean energy and digital payments, which allows diversified funds to capture growth as technology lowers costs and adoption rises. The opportunity persists because governments support energy security, domestic manufacturing and digital infrastructure, while companies continue investing to protect margins and strengthen supply chains.

The main risk is that long-term growth expectations are sensitive to interest rates, so valuations can fall sharply when real yields rise even if revenue continues to grow. Diversification across themes reduces company-specific and policy risk, but it offers less protection when higher discount rates affect all growth assets together. The indicative test returned 12.6% a year with 21.0% volatility, a 46.0% worst fall and return skew of -0.2, showing that losses were larger than a normal distribution would imply.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the Nasdaq Composite’s return during the calendar month. The index provides a consistent measure of growth equity conditions, with large negative readings signalling pressure from weaker earnings expectations, higher discount rates or falling risk appetite.

**October 2008 · Financial crisis · −17.7% · strategy lost**  
Credit stress drives a growth equity sell-off  
Growth equities fell as the financial crisis restricted credit and reduced corporate investment, sending the Nasdaq Composite down 17.7%. The strategy would have lost because automation, digital infrastructure and clean technology all depended on funding and capital spending.

**November 2020 · Policy shift · +11.8% · strategy gained**  
Vaccines and policy support lift growth themes  
Growth equities rose after positive vaccine results and the US election improved expectations for reopening and clean-energy policy. The strategy gained because digital payments retained pandemic-led demand while automation and clean-energy funds benefited from stronger investment expectations.

**February 2022 · Ukraine war · −3.4% · strategy lost**  
War compounds inflation and rate pressure  
Russia’s invasion of Ukraine raised energy costs and added to inflation already driving tighter monetary policy. The strategy lost because higher bond yields reduced growth valuations, while supply disruption increased costs for clean-energy and automation companies.

**August 2022 · Industrial policy · −4.6% · strategy mixed**  
US subsidies support domestic investment themes  
The US enacted major semiconductor and clean-energy subsidies, improving long-term demand for domestic manufacturing and renewable infrastructure. Results were mixed because policy support helped selected themes, but rising real yields drove the broader Nasdaq Composite down 4.6%.

**January 2023 · Disinflation · +10.7% · strategy gained**  
Lower inflation expectations revive growth valuations  
Growth equities rallied as softer inflation improved expectations that monetary tightening would slow. The strategy gained because lower bond yields supported valuations across automation, clean energy and digital payments, lifting the Nasdaq Composite by 10.7%.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +0.9% · strategy mixed**  
Tariffs divide domestic and global themes  
Sweeping US tariff announcements initially reduced risk appetite and raised expected equipment and component costs. Results were mixed because automation and clean-energy supply chains faced margin pressure, while domestic production incentives supported selected companies and the Nasdaq Composite recovered to a small monthly gain.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Falling real yields**: Lower real yields favour the strategy because they increase the present value of earnings expected well into the future. This mechanism supported the broad growth rally in January 2023 as inflation eased and expectations for further monetary tightening moderated.
- **Capital spending accelerates**: Stronger capital spending favours the strategy because automation and infrastructure suppliers gain orders as companies expand capacity and improve productivity. The reopening cycle in 2020 and 2021 supported these themes as manufacturers responded to labour shortages and supply constraints.
- **Policy supports investment**: Industrial subsidies favour the strategy when they improve project economics and create multi-year demand for equipment, software and components. US semiconductor and clean-energy legislation in 2022 strengthened order expectations even though higher interest rates limited near-term equity returns.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Real yields rise sharply**: Higher real yields hurt the strategy because they reduce growth valuations before changes in revenue or earnings become visible. This effect drove the 2022 drawdown as central banks tightened policy and investors reduced exposure to long-duration equities.
- **Subsidies and rules reverse**: Policy reversals hurt the strategy when they reduce expected clean-energy projects or restrict access to important export markets. Changes to solar subsidies and trade rules have previously reduced orders, compressed margins and increased volatility across renewable-energy funds.
- **Theme valuations become crowded**: Crowded valuations hurt the strategy when fund inflows push prices ahead of achievable revenue and earnings growth. Clean-energy and disruptive-technology funds fell sharply after early 2021 as higher yields exposed weak cash flow and excessive valuation assumptions.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Structural Growth?

- **Long-term quality growth equity** (`g1`): Selects durable growth companies directly and bears company-specific execution and valuation risk rather than theme selection risk. *Adds fundamental company selection*
- **Sector rotation strategy** (`m3`): Rotates between sectors using price trends and bears reversal risk when market leadership changes quickly. *Adds tactical control of theme exposure*
- **Minimum volatility equity** (`d5`): Owns lower-volatility equities and bears the risk of lagging when high-growth stocks lead the market. *Reduces broad equity drawdown risk*
