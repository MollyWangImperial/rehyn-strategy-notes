# Barbell bond allocation

`i2` · Income · Government bonds and rates

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Capturing the Term Premium

The investment case rests on the term premium, the extra yield that long-dated government bonds pay for exposing investors to inflation and interest rate risk, while the short-dated half of the portfolio stays liquid and is reinvested at current yields. The premium persists because heavy government borrowing, trade policy and geopolitical tension keep inflation and bond supply uncertain, and few investors will hold long maturities without compensation.

The main risk is a sustained rise in inflation and policy rates, which lowers the price of the long-dated bonds faster than the short-dated bonds can offset. Because the long leg carries most of the portfolio's interest rate sensitivity, a rise of 100 basis points in long yields removes a large part of the income earned over several years. The 2022 rate cycle shows this exposure, as Treasury prices fell while inflation rose and the short leg reinvested at higher yields only after the loss had occurred.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the 10-year US Treasury yield over the episode, in basis points, where 100 basis points equal one percentage point. A fall in yields raises bond prices and a rise lowers them, so the sign shows the direction of the move that mattered for the long-dated bonds.

**December 2008 · Policy easing · −160bp · strategy gained**  
Federal Reserve cuts rates to zero  
The Federal Reserve cut its policy rate to near zero in December 2008, and the 10-year yield fell about 160 basis points over the quarter. The strategy gained because the long-dated bonds rose in price while the short-dated bonds held their value.

**August 2011 · Credit rating · −60bp · strategy gained**  
US credit rating downgrade lowers Treasury yields  
Standard and Poor's cut the US credit rating to AA+ on 5 August 2011, yet the 10-year yield fell about 60 basis points over the month as investors sought safety. The strategy gained because the long-dated bonds rose in price despite the downgrade.

**May 2013 · Fed guidance · +135bp · strategy lost**  
Fed taper signal lifts long-term yields  
The Federal Reserve signalled in May 2013 that it would slow its bond purchases, and the 10-year yield rose about 135 basis points by September. The strategy lost because the long-dated bonds fell faster than the short-dated bonds could offset.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −140bp · strategy mixed**  
Covid first lifts then strains Treasuries  
The 10-year yield fell about 140 basis points from December to 0.54% on 9 March 2020, then Treasury liquidity weakened as investors sold to raise cash. Results were mixed because the long-dated bonds gained early and then gave back part of the gain.

**March 2022 · Rate hikes · +235bp · strategy lost**  
Inflation drives the fastest rate rises in decades  
The Federal Reserve began raising rates in March 2022, and the 10-year yield rose about 235 basis points over the year. The strategy lost most because long-dated bond prices fell sharply while the short leg reinvested at higher yields only gradually.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +48bp · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs push long-term yields higher  
The 10-year yield rose about 48 basis points in the week to 11 April 2025 after the US tariff announcement, as investors sold Treasuries alongside equities. Results were mixed because the long-dated bonds lost value while the short-dated bonds limited the loss.

## Strategy construction, from term premium income to liquidity control

| Position | Instrument | Purpose | Allocation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-year Treasury notes, laddered | US Treasury notes | Provides liquidity and reinvests at current yields | 45% |
| 20 to 30-year Treasury bonds | US Treasury bonds | Earns the term premium and gains when yields fall | 40% |
| 5-year inflation-protected securities | US Treasury inflation-protected securities | Offsets losses when inflation rises | 10% |
| Treasury bills | US Treasury bills | Holds cash for rebalancing and earns the policy rate | 5% |

- Entry: Long leg raised when the 10-year yield is above its five-year average
- Position size: Set to a 5% annualised volatility target
- Exit: Rebalanced quarterly, or when a leg drifts 5 points from its weight
- Stop-loss: Long leg halved if the 10-year yield rises 100 basis points in three months

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn monitors the gap between 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields, inflation breakevens and the size of Treasury auctions, because they show how much compensation investors demand for holding long maturities. Federal Reserve policy meetings, inflation releases and quarterly refunding announcements are the events that most often move the long end. A tariff or energy supply shock that raises inflation expectations would matter more to the long leg than a change in the policy rate.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Falling policy rates and yields**: Falling yields favour the strategy because they raise the price of the long-dated bonds, which carry the most sensitivity to yields. In 2019 the Federal Reserve cut rates three times, and the 10-year yield fell from about 2.7% to 1.9% over the year.
- **Safe haven demand in a crisis**: Safe haven buying favours the strategy because investors move into Treasuries when equity markets fall, which raises long-dated bond prices. In August 2011 the 10-year yield fell about 60 basis points after the US credit rating downgrade, because investors still treated Treasuries as the safest asset.
- **Stable inflation expectations**: Stable inflation expectations favour the strategy because they keep the term premium steady, so long-dated bond prices change little between coupon payments. From 2014 to 2019 US inflation stayed close to 2%, and the strategy collected its coupon income with limited price losses.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Rising inflation and policy rates**: Rising inflation hurts the strategy because investors demand higher yields, which lowers the price of the long-dated bonds by more than the short leg can offset. In 2022 the 10-year yield rose about 235 basis points as the Federal Reserve raised rates, which produced the strategy's deepest drawdown.
- **Higher borrowing and bond supply**: Heavy government borrowing hurts the strategy because investors ask for a higher term premium to absorb the extra supply of long-dated bonds. In October 2023 the 10-year yield reached about 5% after the Treasury raised its auction sizes and the budget deficit widened, which lowered long bond prices even though growth was slowing.
- **Forced selling of Treasuries**: A scramble for cash hurts the strategy because investors sell their most liquid assets, including Treasuries, when other markets come under stress. In March 2020 the 10-year yield rose from 0.5% to about 1.2% within two weeks as Treasury liquidity deteriorated, which erased part of the early gain on the long leg.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Bond Income?

- **Treasury bill laddering** (`i1`): Holds only short bills and earns the policy rate. Bears reinvestment risk when rates fall, with almost no price risk. *Matches this strategy's short leg*
- **Long-duration Treasury hedge** (`d6`): Holds only long-dated Treasuries. Bears duration risk, gaining when growth fears lower yields and losing when inflation rises. *Concentrates this strategy's long leg*
- **Yield curve steepener trade** (`c2`): Positions for the yield curve to steepen. Bears curve risk, losing when short-term yields rise faster than long-term yields. *Earns from curve shape, not yield level*
