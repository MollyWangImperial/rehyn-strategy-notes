# Crypto trend following

`m6` · Momentum · Crypto

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Persistent Crypto Price Trends

The investment case rests on digital asset prices trending for weeks or months, because demand is driven by retail flows, borrowed money and news that spread gradually across markets that trade at all hours. The trends persist because rising prices draw in new buyers and falling prices force leveraged holders to sell, while regulatory and policy decisions, such as fund approvals and trading bans, set the direction for long periods.

The main risk is a sharp reversal after the strategy has entered a trend, because digital assets can fall 30% or more within days. The strategy exits only after the trend turns, so it gives back part of each decline and then misses part of the rebound that follows. Exchange failures, hacks and regulatory action can also cause price gaps that no exit rule can avoid.

Regulatory decisions, court rulings and central bank liquidity announcements are the events most likely to start or end a trend. Rising volatility signals a higher risk of reversal.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Regulatory decisions that open demand**: Clear regulatory approval favours the strategy because it brings in institutional buyers who build positions over months. The approval of spot Bitcoin funds in January 2024 began an uptrend of about 58% in two months.
- **Rising liquidity and risk appetite**: Easier monetary conditions favour the strategy because investors move into higher-risk assets and borrow more to hold them. After the Fed cut rates to near zero in March 2020, Bitcoin rose from about 5,000 dollars to about 64,000 dollars by April 2021.
- **Orderly, gradual bear markets**: A long, gradual decline favours the strategy because the trend filter moves to cash after the first fall and avoids much of the remaining loss. In 2018, Bitcoin fell about 84% from its peak, and the strategy held cash for most of the decline.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Sudden exchange failures and hacks**: Failures of exchanges and lenders hurt the strategy when it holds a position, because prices gap lower before any exit rule can act. In November 2022, the collapse of FTX cut Bitcoin by about 25% in two weeks.
- **Regulatory bans and reversals**: Sudden regulatory action hurts the strategy when it ends a rally that the trend filter was following. In 2021, China's ban on mining and trading, together with Tesla's decision to stop accepting Bitcoin, cut the price by about 53% over three months.
- **Sharp rebounds after sell-offs**: Fast rebounds hurt the strategy because it is in cash when a recovery starts and re-enters only after the price has risen. After the March 2020 fall, Bitcoin rose by about 70% within seven weeks, and the strategy re-entered after much of that gain.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Crypto Premiums?

- **Core digital asset allocation** (`g4`): Holds a rebalanced core of the largest digital assets. Bears drawdown risk, losing in long bear markets. *Always invested, with no trend filter*
- **Crypto basis trading** (`r2`): Earns the gap between spot and futures prices. Bears exchange and counterparty risk, losing if a platform fails. *Market neutral, with little price direction risk*
- **Crypto volatility arbitrage** (`s7`): Trades implied against realised volatility in crypto options. Bears liquidity risk, losing when option markets become illiquid. *Paid for volatility, not for direction*
