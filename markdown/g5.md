# Pre-IPO growth equity

`g5` · Growth · Private and structured

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Funding Late-Stage Growth

The investment case rests on buying late-stage private companies before public investors can access them, allowing capital to earn from revenue growth, operational scale and the valuation uplift that often accompanies a successful listing. The opportunity persists because private companies accept an illiquidity discount in exchange for patient capital, while listing rules, industrial policy and geopolitical controls restrict both the timing of exits and the supply of institutional funding.

The main risk is that public growth multiples fall before an exit, forcing private valuations lower even when revenue continues to rise. Losses can deepen because funding rounds become less frequent, weaker companies accept punitive terms and IPO proceeds remain unavailable for several years. The estimated 15.2% annual return therefore comes with 24.0% volatility, a 48.0% worst fall and negative return skew, so reported quarterly values understate the speed of economic losses.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the Nasdaq Composite’s calendar-year percentage change during the episode. The index is a useful measure of the public growth valuation backdrop because technology and other high-growth companies account for much of its market value.

**September 2008 · Financial crisis · −41% · strategy lost**  
Lehman failure closes the listing market  
The financial crisis closed the US IPO market as investors reduced equity risk and funding conditions tightened. The strategy lost because prospective exits were delayed, revenue forecasts weakened and late-stage valuations were marked against sharply lower public multiples.

**April 2012 · Policy · +16% · strategy mixed**  
JOBS Act broadens access to public markets  
The US JOBS Act reduced disclosure requirements for emerging growth companies and made public listings easier. Results were mixed because the policy improved the exit route, while Facebook’s weak post-IPO performance showed that private valuations could still exceed public demand.

**December 2018 · Trade policy · −4% · strategy lost**  
Trade tensions reset late-stage valuations  
US-China trade tensions and tighter monetary policy drove a sharp fourth-quarter decline in public growth shares. The strategy lost because investors cut peer multiples and applied larger discounts to companies exposed to Chinese demand, supply chains or cross-border regulation.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · +44% · strategy gained**  
Policy support reopens the exit window  
The pandemic initially stopped listings, but fiscal support and near-zero interest rates drove public growth valuations sharply higher. The strategy gained because digital businesses accelerated revenue growth and the IPO market reopened at stronger multiples later in the year.

**November 2021 · IPO boom · +21% · strategy gained**  
Cheap capital drives record listings  
Low interest rates and strong demand for growth shares produced a record year for US listings and venture-backed exits. The strategy gained because portfolio companies accessed public markets at high revenue multiples, converting private valuation gains into realised proceeds.

**February 2022 · War · −33% · strategy lost**  
Rate rises close the IPO window  
Russia’s invasion of Ukraine intensified inflation pressure as central banks began rapid rate rises and public growth shares fell. The strategy lost because the IPO window closed, funding terms tightened and private valuations followed listed peers lower with a delay.

- Position size: Single-company exposure capped at 8% and each expected listing year at 25%
- Exit: Position realised through an IPO, secondary sale or acquisition within five to seven years
- Stop-loss: New funding suspended if the listed peer basket falls 25% from its peak

### Today’s market watch

Public growth multiples and the pace of new IPO filings are the main indicators because they determine both private valuations and access to exits. Central bank guidance, competition policy, semiconductor controls and changes to listing rules matter most when they alter funding costs or restrict cross-border capital.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Public growth multiples rise**: Rising public growth multiples favour the strategy because private funding rounds and IPO prices are set against listed peers. In 2020, strong technology equity performance lifted late-stage valuations and reopened the listing market after the initial pandemic closure.
- **The IPO window broadens**: A broad IPO market favours the strategy because more portfolio companies can realise gains without accepting a large liquidity discount. In 2021, record US issuance allowed venture-backed companies to complete exits at high revenue multiples.
- **Revenue growth exceeds forecasts**: Strong operating performance favours the strategy because companies can grow into earlier valuations even when market multiples remain stable. Pandemic-era demand for cloud software and digital commerce produced this outcome for many late-stage companies in 2020 and 2021.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Public multiples contract sharply**: Falling public multiples hurt the strategy because private companies are valued against listed peers despite less frequent reporting. In 2022, the Nasdaq Composite fell 33%, leading to down rounds and substantial valuation cuts across late-stage portfolios.
- **The IPO window closes**: A closed IPO market hurts the strategy because expected exits are delayed while companies continue to consume cash. After the financial crisis in 2008 and the rate shock in 2022, issuers postponed listings and relied on more expensive private funding.
- **Funding terms become punitive**: Tighter funding conditions hurt the strategy because new investors demand liquidation preferences, senior claims or lower valuations. During the 2023 banking stress, venture-backed companies faced reduced credit availability after Silicon Valley Bank failed.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Private-Market Returns?

- **Private equity secondaries** (`v8`): Buys seasoned private fund interests at discounts and bears manager, valuation and exit-timing risk. *Earlier liquidity, lower growth exposure*
- **Direct lending** (`i7`): Provides senior private loans and earns income for bearing default and illiquidity risk. *Same market, higher capital priority*
- **Autocallable note laddering** (`c8`): Earns structured coupons while bearing issuer credit risk and losses after large equity declines. *Public-market route to structured growth exposure*
