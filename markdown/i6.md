# Real asset income investing

`i6` · Income · Real assets

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Contracted Real Asset Income

The investment case rests on property, utility and infrastructure assets that collect contracted rents and regulated tariffs, so their income is more predictable than the earnings of most companies. The income persists because new assets are slow and costly to build, licences and regulation limit competition, and many contracts pass inflation through to revenue, which matters when energy prices or trade policy raise costs.

The main risk is a rise in real interest rates, because the value of long-lived cash flows falls when the discount rate rises. Income can stay stable while asset values fall sharply, as in 2022 when listed property fell as yields rose even though rents continued to grow. Debt adds to the risk, because owners that must refinance at higher rates pass part of their income to lenders.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows the change in the 10-year US Treasury yield over the episode, in basis points, where 100 basis points equal one percentage point. Real assets are valued by discounting long-lived income, so a rise in yields usually lowers their prices and a fall raises them.

**May 2013 · Fed guidance · +135bp · strategy lost**  
Taper signal lifts yields and hits real assets  
The Federal Reserve signalled in May 2013 that it would slow bond purchases, and the 10-year yield rose about 135 basis points by September. The strategy lost because higher yields lowered the value of rent and utility cash flows.

**July 2016 · Brexit · −90bp · strategy gained**  
Brexit pushes yields to record lows  
The 10-year yield fell about 90 basis points from December 2015 to a record low of 1.4% in July 2016, after the United Kingdom voted to leave the European Union. The strategy gained because lower yields raised the value of stable rental and regulated income.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −140bp · strategy lost**  
Covid sell-off hits property and infrastructure  
The 10-year yield fell about 140 basis points from December to 9 March 2020, but listed property and infrastructure shares fell sharply as tenants lost income. The strategy lost because lower yields did not offset the risk of unpaid rent and weaker traffic volumes.

**March 2022 · Rate hikes · +235bp · strategy lost**  
Rate rises lower the value of property assets  
The Federal Reserve began raising rates in March 2022, and the 10-year yield rose about 235 basis points over the year. The strategy lost because higher yields lowered property values, although inflation-linked rents and regulated tariffs limited the fall in income.

**December 2023 · Policy pivot · −110bp · strategy gained**  
Fed signals cuts and yields fall sharply  
The 10-year yield fell about 110 basis points from its October peak to the end of 2023 after the Federal Reserve signalled rate cuts. The strategy gained because lower yields raised the value of property and infrastructure cash flows.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · +48bp · strategy mixed**  
US tariffs raise costs and lift yields  
The 10-year yield rose about 48 basis points in the week to 11 April 2025 as tariffs raised inflation concerns. Results were mixed because infrastructure with inflation-linked revenue held its value, while property shares fell with higher yields.

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn monitors the 10-year real yield, the gap between asset income yields and government bond yields, and net debt against earnings, because these show how sensitive asset values are to financing costs. Central bank rate decisions, inflation releases and changes in energy or trade policy are the events that matter most. A rise in real yields would weigh on valuations, while contracted income would continue to support cash flow.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Falling real yields**: Falling real yields favour the strategy because lower discount rates raise the value of long-lived income. After the Federal Reserve signalled rate cuts in December 2023, the 10-year yield fell about 110 basis points from its October peak and listed property rallied strongly.
- **Inflation-linked revenue**: Inflation pass-through favours the strategy because rents, tariffs and tolls rise with prices, which protects income when costs increase. In 2022 US consumer prices rose about 6.5% over the year and regulated utilities and toll road operators raised revenue, so infrastructure held up better than the equity market.
- **Stable credit conditions**: Stable credit conditions favour the strategy because owners refinance debt at manageable rates, so income reaches investors instead of lenders. Between 2012 and 2019 low borrowing costs allowed property and infrastructure owners to extend debt maturities and raise distributions.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Rising real interest rates**: Rising real yields hurt the strategy because higher discount rates lower the present value of long-lived income. In 2022 the 10-year yield rose about 235 basis points and listed property fell sharply even though rents continued to grow.
- **Tenant stress and lockdowns**: A sudden loss of tenant income hurts the strategy because rent and traffic revenue can stop while debt payments continue. In March 2020 retail, hotel and airport assets lost income as lockdowns began, so listed real assets fell sharply.
- **Tighter credit and refinancing**: Tighter credit hurts the strategy because owners that must refinance debt pay higher interest, which absorbs income that would otherwise be distributed. After the 2013 taper signal, borrowing costs rose and listed property and utilities fell as higher yields fed through to refinancing costs.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Contracted Income?

- **Inflation-linked infrastructure investing** (`c9`): Owns infrastructure with revenue linked to inflation. Bears regulatory and political risk, with returns tied to price growth. *Narrower focus on inflation-linked income*
- **REIT discount to NAV investing** (`v7`): Buys listed property below appraised value. Bears the risk that the discount persists or widens when yields rise. *Earns from the discount, not the income*
- **Investment grade bond laddering** (`i3`): Lends to high-quality companies for fixed coupons. Bears credit and interest rate risk, with no ownership of assets. *Contracted income from lending, not ownership*
