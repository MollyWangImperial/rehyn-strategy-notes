# Cross-asset trend following

`m1` · Momentum · Futures

## Why the opportunity exists
### The Structural Opportunity: Persistent Cross-Asset Trends

The investment case rests on the tendency of large price moves in equities, bonds, currencies and commodities to continue for weeks or months, because central banks, governments and large investors change their positions gradually rather than all at once. The pattern persists because policy responses are slow and portfolio changes are often delayed, and geopolitical and policy shocks that reshape inflation, growth and interest rate expectations tend to produce multi-month trends rather than one-day jumps.

The main risk is a sharp reversal after a trend is established, which can erase several weeks of gains within days. Losses are most likely when markets move sideways or when a central bank or government decision reverses a trend that has attracted heavy positioning. The strategy can therefore lag for long periods, and its protection in equity sell-offs depends on those sell-offs developing gradually rather than reversing quickly.

## What Real Market Shocks Reveal About This Strategy

The large figure on each card shows how far the S&P 500 moved during the episode, over the period described in the text. Trend following earns from sustained moves in either direction, so the size of the move shows how much trend there was to follow.

**October 2008 · Financial crisis · −17% · strategy gained**  
Lehman collapse drives a long equity decline  
The S&P 500 fell about 17% in October 2008 as credit markets froze after the failure of Lehman Brothers. The strategy gained because short equity and long Treasury positions were already in place and the trends continued for months.

**March 2009 · Policy rescue · +36% · strategy lost**  
Rescue measures reverse the crisis trends  
From 9 March to the end of May 2009, the S&P 500 rose about 36% as government rescue measures stabilised the banks. The strategy lost because its short equity and long bond positions reversed together.

**March 2020 · Pandemic · −34% · strategy mixed**  
Covid sell-off reverses within weeks  
The S&P 500 fell about 34% between 19 February and 23 March 2020 as Covid closures spread, before emergency Fed and government support started a rapid recovery. Results were mixed because short equity gains were partly offset by losses when markets reversed.

**January 2022 · Rate rises · −25% · strategy gained**  
Fed rate rises start a year-long trend  
From January to October 2022 the S&P 500 fell about 25% as the Fed raised rates to control inflation and bond prices fell with equities. The strategy gained because short bond and equity positions followed the move for most of the year.

**August 2024 · Carry trade · −8.5% · strategy lost**  
Bank of Japan rate rise reverses carry positions  
The S&P 500 fell about 8.5% between 16 July and 5 August 2024 after a Bank of Japan rate rise and weak US jobs data unwound yen-funded trades. The strategy lost because long equity and short yen positions reversed within days.

**April 2025 · Trade policy · −19% · strategy lost**  
US tariffs reverse established trends  
The S&P 500 fell about 19% from 19 February to 8 April 2025 as US tariffs cut growth forecasts, before a pause in tariffs prompted a rebound. The strategy lost because dollar and equity trends reversed within days.

### Today’s market watch

Rehyn watches whether price trends point the same way across the four asset groups, because trends that agree in several markets tend to last longer. Realised volatility in bond and equity futures matters because it sets position size and the risk of a reversal. Central bank meetings, tariff decisions and elections are the events most likely to change an established trend at short notice.

## Performance drivers and the geopolitical risks that matter most

**Favourable conditions**

- **Gradual, persistent policy cycles**: Interest rate cycles that unfold in steps favour the strategy because yields and currencies keep moving in one direction for many months. In 2022, successive Fed rate rises lifted the US dollar and pushed bond prices down in a steady trend that the strategy followed in both.
- **Prolonged equity sell-offs**: Equity declines that develop over weeks favour the strategy because it can sell equity futures and buy government bond futures as investors move into safer assets. In the 2008 financial crisis, this positioning produced large gains as the S&P 500 fell by more than half and Treasury yields declined.
- **Supply-driven commodity moves**: Supply disruptions favour the strategy because producers and consumers take months to adjust, so prices keep moving after the first reaction. Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine pushed oil, gas and wheat prices higher for several months, which rewarded long positions.

**Adverse conditions**

- **Sharp reversals after policy surprises**: Policy surprises hurt the strategy when they reverse a trend that has attracted heavy positioning, leaving it on the wrong side of several markets at once. In August 2024, a Bank of Japan rate rise reversed long equity and short yen positions within days.
- **Range-bound markets without direction**: Markets that move sideways hurt the strategy because trend signals reverse repeatedly, so each new position loses a small amount through reversals and trading costs. Trend followers struggled in 2016 for this reason, as oil and equity prices reversed several times within the year.
- **Rebounds after severe sell-offs**: Sharp recoveries hurt the strategy because it holds short equity and long bond positions when markets turn upward. In March 2009, the S&P 500 began a rally of about 36% over three months, and the strategy lost on those defensive positions.

## Where Else Can Investors Find Trend Premiums?

- **Equity momentum investing** (`m2`): Holds the shares with the strongest recent returns. Bears momentum crash risk, losing when market leadership reverses sharply. *Same signal, applied to individual shares*
- **Global macro futures overlay** (`c7`): Takes directional views on growth, inflation and policy. Bears forecast risk, losing when the economic outlook is misjudged. *Macro views rather than price trends*
- **Long volatility hedging** (`d4`): Holds volatility futures that rise in stress. Bears roll cost, paying in calm markets and gaining in a crash. *Offsets this strategy's reversal months*
